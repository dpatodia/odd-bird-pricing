Weaknesses and loopholes in this model

Read these before acting on the numbers. Each one could move the recommendation.

  1. The demand model starts from guesses. Its starting assumptions (about 4% of visits convert at the target price; elasticity near 1.8) were chosen by Claude, not measured. Until several weeks of data arrive, its output is those guesses restated.
  2. The demand curve has a fixed shape. The model assumes the same elasticity at every price from ₹399 to ₹999, so its view of prices far from the ones tested is unreliable. A coupon is treated as a plain price cut, which it is not.
  3. The demand model reads all sales changes as price effects. Ads, search rank, new reviews, stock-outs and sale events also move conversion week to week.
  4. Market data for the Large Shopper and Toiletry bag is thin. Amazon search does not separate totes by size, so "large" comparables are totes whose titles say large; their real sizes were not checked. The 16 toiletry listings are mostly nylon organisers, a different product from a padded printed cotton pouch.
  5. It shows profit per order, not total profit. The model has no view of how many orders each price brings. ₹799 earns more per order than ₹699, but if it sells half as many units it earns less in total. Only a live price test settles this.
  6. Fee rates are second-hand. They come from two websites that read Amazon's rate card in September and November 2026, not from Seller Central. Handbags have no row of their own in the referral table read; the 19% and the ₹1,000 threshold are taken from the wider fashion and luggage group. If the rate or threshold differs for this browse node, the ceiling and the "dead zone" move.
  7. The demand signal is coarse. "Bought in past month" is a floor shown in steps (50+, 100+, 200+). Listings with no badge may sell 0 or 49. The badge can also belong to a whole variation family, not the one colour shown. Best-seller rank and price history were not collected.
  8. The competitors are not like for like. Sizes and features were not captured. Most ₹700 to 900 sellers are larger shoulder totes with a zip and pockets. The Beetle Medium is a 30 cm hand-carry open-top bag, closer in use to the ₹349 to 499 lunch totes. The model does not adjust for this.
  9. The sample is small, hand-picked and from one day. 64 listings chosen by judgment from 28 searches on 16 November 2026. Prices on a search page can be deal or festive prices. A different day or a different set of searches could shift the bands.
  10. Ad spend and return rate are guesses. 15% and 8% are placeholders. A new listing with no reviews often spends far more on ads per order in its first weeks than a settled one. Ads are modelled as a flat share of price, which hides this.
  11. Returns are treated kindly. Every returned bag is assumed to go back on sale at full value. Damaged or unsellable returns, and any return-processing charge, are left out.
  12. The cost figure is unverified. The costs are used as given. It is not known whether it includes packaging, the MRP label, freight to the Amazon warehouse or factory overhead, or whether it is a transfer price from Bag Studio. Storage, removal and long-term storage fees are also left out.
  13. Shipping weight is assumed. The bags weigh well under 500 g, but Amazon charges the higher of actual and volumetric weight. A padded bag packed 30 × 30 × 3 cm counts as 540 g. The slabs used (Toiletry up to 500 g; Medium and Large 500 g to 1 kg) are guesses until packed sizes are measured. A wrong slab moves profit by ₹20 to 35 an order. The real slab depends on packed size (length × width × height in cm ÷ 5,000). No local-delivery rate was found, so regional is used for all non-national orders.
  14. The MRP follows the crowd. ₹1,699 matches what competitors print. That is evidence of a norm, not of legal safety: their MRPs may be inflated too. The MRP must be printed on the pack and be the same on every channel. Rules taking effect on 1 January 2027 require a prior price beside an announced price cut, and how they treat MRP strike-throughs is unsettled.
  15. Coupons, deals and sale events are not modelled. Lightning deals and festival sales need a further 10 to 20% cut, which comes straight out of the profit shown.
  16. The recommendation is a judgment. The price range, target and launch price were chosen by reading the data, not calculated by it. They rest on one size from a brand with no reviews yet.
  17. Fees change. Amazon India revised its fees in March and again in September 2026. The numbers here will go stale.

How profit is calculated

Profit per order shipped = (1 − return rate) × (price without GST − referral fee − closing fee − product cost) − weight handling − pick and pack − return shipping on returned orders − ad spend. GST on Amazon's fees is left out because it is claimable as input credit.

Sources for fees: Zoutons reading of Amazon's rate card (5 Nov 2026); Blooprint fee guide (rates checked 16 Sep 2026); GST Council 56th meeting for the 5% rate on cotton hand bags (HSN 4202 22 20).
